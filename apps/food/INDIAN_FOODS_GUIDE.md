# Indian foods in NutriTrace - quick guide

## 1. Load the starter list (one time, ~2 minutes)

The file `indian-foods-starter.csv` (in this folder) has **81 common Indian foods** - roti, dosa, idli,
dal, rice, sabzi, paneer, chicken, chai, snacks, sweets, ghee and so on.

1. Open NutriTrace (OpenGym -> **Food** tab, or https://levelup.tail7782ec.ts.net/food/).
2. Go to **Settings -> Import & Export -> Bulk Import**.
3. Choose `indian-foods-starter.csv` and look at the preview. It should say 81 foods and no errors.
4. Confirm the import. The foods land in your **Local** catalog.
5. Try it: in the diary tap **+ Add**, search "roti" or "dal", and use the **Local** filter chip.

**These numbers are approximate.** Every row has the brand "approx." so you can tell them apart. They are
typical values for one common serving (1 roti, 1 katori of dal, 1 cup of chai...), not lab-measured, and
home cooking varies a lot (oil, ghee, portion size). When you know better, correct them: open the food and
edit it. Nothing here was copied from a protected database.

## 2. How servings work

Each food is **one serving** (the name says which: "Roti (1 medium)", "Dal tadka (1 katori)").
When you log it, **quantity = number of servings**. 2 rotis = quantity 2. A bigger bowl = quantity 1.5.
(1 katori = a small bowl, roughly 150 ml. Use your own katori if it is different.)

## 3. Your own recipes and "my usual meals"

- **Save a usual meal in two taps:** log the foods you normally eat together (say 2 roti + dal + sabzi) in a
  meal slot. Then open that meal's **menu (the three dots)** and pick **Save meal to library**. Next time, it
  appears in the **+ Add** search - pick it and the whole thing is logged.
- **Make a recipe:** create a Recipe from ingredients and set how many servings it makes (the "yield").
  Logging 1 serving then uses the right per-portion numbers. Use **Split Recipe** if you want to change one
  ingredient of something you already logged.
- **Eat the same thing again:** open the meal's menu -> **Copy meal to another date**, and choose today.
  For a single item, long-press it (phone) or right-click it (PC) -> **Copy to another date**.
- **Fix a mistake:** long-press / right-click an entry -> **Edit**.

## 4. Tips

- Search packaged food by name or scan the barcode - Open Food Facts is good for that. For home-cooked food,
  use the Local list above. (Open Food Facts mostly returns dry packet mixes for words like "idli", which
  would give you wrong calories for a cooked idli.)
- Add anything you eat often as your own food once, and it is there forever.
